# Market, Validation, Business Model and Pitch

## Core segments
- Families and parents buying creative experiences/keepsakes.
- Seniors and hobbyist musicians completing songs they've carried for years.
- Kids exploring musical creativity with guardians.
- Adults who sing or compose without instruments/studio expertise.
- Amateur songwriters, faith/community musicians, teachers.

## Market thesis
Most music software assumes instruments, production expertise or text prompting. Songbird starts with natural human sounds and helps users complete, preserve and share them. This is a positioning hypothesis, not proof of unique capability; benchmark competitors such as Suno, Udio, BandLab and hum-to-music products.

## Pricing hypotheses
Free creation; per-song studio exports, premium stems/mastering, video packages and family subscription for storage/collaboration. Illustrative package prices in `product/MAGIC_LOG_AND_PACKAGES.md`; do not hardcode as final.

## Validation sequence
1. Test with 10–20 participants across audience segments; ask them to record clap+hum and finish without coaching.
2. Benchmark perceived audio quality and fidelity vs source; identify where Magic Fix helps vs damages sound.
3. Private beta 25–50 creators: completion, return, sharing, willingness to pay, compute cost and support burden.
4. Launch paid exports only after unit economics and rights checks; then test video add-ons.

## Pitch story
'Everyone has a song inside them. Songbird helps bring it to life.' Demo: senior hums -> clap loop -> guitar/drum transformation -> one-tap cleanup -> master -> family video -> private share. Highlight preservation of original performance, adaptive AI producer, non-destructive layers, cross-generational UX, and data from real usage.

## Funding note
Build a credible prototype while employed subject to employment/IP review. Seek pre-seed capital after demonstrating repeatable high-quality transformations and user demand; fundraising ranges are not guaranteed.
