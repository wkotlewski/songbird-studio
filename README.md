# Songbird

Copyright © 2026 Whitney Kotlewski. All rights reserved.

**For anyone with a hum, or a song in their heart.**

Songbird is a music studio that fits in your pocket. You bring whatever you've got: a hum, a sung line, a clap, a beatbox, a few chords on a guitar. Songbird helps you build it into a song you'd be proud to play for anyone.

You don't need to play an instrument. You don't need to know what a compressor is, or how to set up a mix. Sing the melody and Songbird can play it back as piano, guitar, bass, choir, horns, or drums. Pick a sound from the 1920s, the '70s, or today. Blend genres: gospel chords under an R&B groove, a parlor piano behind a hip-hop beat. You make the music. Songbird does the studio work.

**Open Songbird:** [wkotlewski.github.io/songbird-studio](https://wkotlewski.github.io/songbird-studio/)

## Like a loop pedal, without the learning curve

Watch someone perform with a loop pedal: they tap a beat on the guitar body, and it loops. They hum a bass line on top, and that loops too. Then chords, then a harmony, and then they sing over a whole band they built in front of you, one layer at a time.

That's the feeling Songbird is built around, for everyone, not only people who own the gear.

1. **Tap record.** Songbird counts you in: 1, 2, 3, 4.
2. **Make a sound.** Clap, hum, beatbox, or play for 2, 4, or 8 bars. You can also record until you tap stop.
3. **It loops.** Songbird closes the loop on the beat and plays it back right away, over and over.
4. **Stack the next layer.** Tap record again, and it comes in from the top, in time with what's already there.
5. **Make it magic.** Turn your hum into piano chords, your claps into a drum kit, your voice into a choir. Then mix it, and download the song.

## Just say what you want

Editing a photo with AI is easy now: you say “remove the background” and it's done. Songbird brings that to music. Open **✨ Magic** and type (or speak, with your keyboard's mic) what you want:

- “Make it tight” snaps every layer to the beat and fixes the pitch.
- “Remove background noise”
- “Add a horn section”, “add harmony vocals”, “add drums”, “add a bass line”, “add strings”
- “Turn my humming into a trumpet”
- “Make it higher”, “put it in G”, “transpose down an octave”
- “Bring up the vocals”, “turn down the guitar”, “remove the drums”
- “A little faster”, “slow it down”
- “Fade out at the end”, “more reverb”
- “Make it sound like the 70s”, “make it gospel”

You can ask for several things at once: “add drums and make it tight”. Every change can be undone, and your original recording is always kept.

You can also pick a sound yourself: drums (a full kit that follows your claps, or one sound such as congas or a tambourine), piano, organ, acoustic or electric guitar, bass, violin, cello, a string section, trumpet, sax, a horn section, flute, a choir or a solo voice, marimba, bells, steel drums, kalimba, sitar, synths, and more. Choose how it plays (solo, chords, or a full section) and the era it comes from.

Kids can just play. Grandparents can hum the hymn they've carried for fifty years. Musicians can sketch an idea in a minute, before it's gone.

## Who it's for

- **Kids** who want to make noise and hear it turn into music
- **Seniors and families** keeping old melodies and family songs alive
- **Hobbyists** with unfinished songs and no studio
- **Musicians** who want a quick sketchpad for a loop, a groove, or an arrangement

Songbird never guesses what you can do from your age. Big, clear controls are there for everyone, and the deeper controls are there when you want them.

## What you can do

My Music and Studio are the only two places. Everything else opens over your song.

- **Record:** use the microphone with the loop pedal, or start from a vibe: claps, snaps, guitar strums, or piano chords in the key you pick. A mic level control and meter show how loud you're coming in before and while you record.
- **Play an instrument:** tap piano keys or piano chords, strum guitar chords (the ones that fit your key, or every chord: pick a letter, then major, minor, seven, sus, and more), or play a drum kit on pads, right on the screen (or with a computer keyboard). Songbird counts you in, plays along, and puts every note on the beat.
- **Check your notes:** before a hum becomes an instrument, Songbird shows the notes and the key it heard. Pick the right key, move every note higher or lower, tap a key to fix one note, or turn a note into a chord. The instrument plays exactly what you approved, and you can open the notes again any time.
- **Make it a full song:** once you have a groove, say what the song is for (build like a soul song, a song to sing over, a groove to jam on, a big finish, background music) and pick a few words for how it should feel, or just say it: “steady, with background vocals and a guitar solo”. Songbird adds what's missing, such as drums, bass, chords, background vocals, a solo, strings, or horns. It lays the song out in sections like verse, chorus, and bridge, brings parts in and out, adds drum fills into the big moments, and fades it. Color comes in just when it's needed, picked for the vibe: a shaker from the second verse, congas and tambourine in the choruses, a harp sweep into each chorus, a flute line over verse two, a vibraphone sparkle on the last chorus. One Undo takes it all back. Each feel has a few sounds for each job (chords, solo, drums, bass, background vocals, guitar), so every build brings in a different band from the last one; Shuffle the sounds picks another before you build.
- **My Music cards:** Share and a ⋮ menu sit on the right of every song card, lined up. Delete is inside the ⋮ menu, and asks once more before it removes anything, so a song can't be deleted by a stray tap.
- **Magic, all in view:** Magic says what it works on: the whole song, or just the layer you picked. Its cards change with that choice. For the whole song: Make it a full song, Add an instrument, Music style, Clean up every recording, Make it studio quality, Make my vocal shine. For one layer: Fix this layer, Change its sound, Make my vocal shine, Tune it, Clean it up, Back to original, Its volume and echo. Whatever you ask for follows the same choice. Building a song always works on the whole song and says so. The guide's Make the whole song sound great step always works on the whole song.
- **Mixer:** the place to make the whole song sound finished. Make it studio quality lists the steps it does, in order: Remove hiss, Balance everything, Make room for my voice, Final tone (Natural, Warm, Bright, Punchy, or Airy for the whole song, with low and top end to fine-tune), Polish (glue and a ceiling, so it's louder without clipping). Turn any step off, or tap Just this to do one; Do all steps runs them with one Undo, and each step gets a check when done. Tighten timing sits apart, since it changes your takes. The tone and polish are in the WAV download too. Each sound has its level and side, mute, hear only this, its tone, and ⋯ for everything else you can do with it.
- **History:** every change you make while the app is open is kept, named by what it did, with the time. Tap the clock above the song (or hold Undo) to see them all, newest first, and go back to before any of them in one tap; changes you went back past stay in the list, and you can bring them back.
- **My Music:** each song shows its name first (up to two lines), one line about it (length and layers, or who sent it), and a little wave drawn from its own sound. Share and Delete are small buttons on the right.
- **Lane symbols:** a wand marks a layer made with Magic, a microphone one you recorded.
- **Pick something, then see what you can do with it:** tap a part of the song (like Chorus) and every layer playing in it is picked; tap a layer to leave it out or bring it in, then Take out here, Bring back here, Play this part, or Record in this part. Tap a layer instead and everything you can do with it shows right under that layer: Make my vocal shine, Fix this layer, Volume and echo, Magic, Change the sound, Length and fades, Back to original, Delete. Done unpicks.
- **Panels on a phone:** a panel fills the screen between the player controls and the bottom buttons, so play, stop, record, and undo always stay in reach, and the panel's title and Close stay at the top however far you scroll.
- **No surprise zooming:** tapping a part's name picks the part (rename it in Mix, under Name this sound); a quick double tap doesn't zoom the page, and typing in a box doesn't zoom in on iPhone.
- **Back to original:** any recording you've changed can go back to just as you recorded it, in one tap: the history button on its strip in Mix, Back to original in Your vocal or Magic, or say “back to the original” or “remove all the effects”. The take you recorded comes back (before timing, studio sound, hiss removal, pitch, or any Magic change), and its echo, room, tone, and the band stepping back for it are turned off. Its volume and place in the song stay. One Undo brings the changes back.
- **Make your vocal shine:** record yourself singing, then open Your vocal (from Magic, or the voice button in Mix). Songbird listens first and tells you what it hears: your key and range, how many lines you sang, whether you slide into notes like a soul singer, and whether your lines land early or late. Then it suggests what fits. Background singers come as four ideas, ordered for your voice, to tap and compare with the song: Soul oohs (real choir oohs holding the band's chords, with your voice doubled left and right), Choir answers (a choir singing in the spaces between your lines), Your harmonies (your own voice, moved note by note to the band's chord a third or so above you and below you, keeping your slides, vibrato, and words; it works one cycle of your voice at a time, so it still sounds like you instead of a robot), and Just you, doubled (two natural doubles of your own take). On the beat slides each line you sang, whole, to where it fits the beat, so nothing inside your singing is stretched. Studio sound and Sit on top of the music make your voice clear and let the band step back while you sing. Do it all does every step with the best-fit singers, with one Undo. You can say it too: “add some oohs behind my vocal”, “call and response background vocals”, “line up my vocal with the beat”.
- **Fix a part:** if a part Songbird added doesn't work, pick it (tap its lane, then Magic, or the tune button in Mix) and say what's off: the notes, the rhythm or riff, the sound, or too busy. Try another take plays it another way: a new strum or groove, simpler chords that change less, a different voicing or octave, a nearby instrument (a harp becomes a nylon guitar, a shaker a tambourine), or fewer notes. Less of it takes it out of the sections that need it least and keeps it for the big moments; More of it brings it back. You can say it too: “the guitar riff is weird”, “less congas”, “more horns”, “the choir notes sound off”. Each change is one Undo.
- **Remove hiss:** phone mics hiss. Songbird learns each recording's steady hiss from its quietest moments and takes it out everywhere, under your voice too, without dulling it. Do it for one layer or the whole song in Magic, and new recordings are cleaned as you record (you can turn that off).
- **Build:** set how long the whole song is, or just one layer, and fade either in and out. Keep it steady, build it up to a climax, or go verse and chorus.
- **✨ Magic:** say what you want, or turn a layer into one of 40 real recorded instruments, 7 drum kits, and 11 percussion sounds. Choose the era: 1900s parlor, 1920s, '50s, '60s, '70s, '80s, '90s, 2000s, or today. Add a genre package, from 25 including gospel, R&B, hip-hop, country, jazz, Afrobeats, lo-fi, cinematic, and children's music. Or blend two together. Magic clear tidies hiss and brings a voice forward.
- **Mix:** set each layer's volume, bass, treble, left and right, and space, or pick a mix preset. Magic timing tightens notes that are barely off the beat.
- **Finish:** download the song as a WAV file.
- **Send a song:** in Save, or Share on a song in My Music, tap Send the song file. Every layer, the mix, the sections, the speed, and the key go into one `.songbird` file you can send by text, email, or AirDrop. Whoever gets it taps Open a song file in My Music (or drops it on the page on a computer), and the song is in their list, ready to play and keep building on. It's their own copy: what they change stays on their device, and yours stays as it is.
- **Undo** steps back through any edit. Your original recording is always kept, so you can go back.

Everything stays on your device. Your songs and recordings are not uploaded anywhere. A song only leaves when you send the file yourself.

## Still growing

Songbird is being built in the open, one phase at a time. When a feature isn't fully working yet, the studio says so instead of pretending. Magic understands requests on your device, using the common ways people ask for things. It doesn't send your words or your music anywhere, and when it doesn't understand, it says so and suggests what it can do. Coming next: tools to mark and erase a single sound in a take, and live instrument monitoring while you sing.

## For engineers

The public page is a static site: HTML, CSS, and JavaScript, hosted on GitHub Pages. There is no application framework and no server.

Sound runs in the browser with the Web Audio API. The microphone uses the MediaRecorder API. Building instruments and genre packages runs in a Web Worker, so the studio keeps playing and scrolling while a layer is being built. A looped layer is built for one pass and then repeated. Song titles and recordings stay in this browser. A `.songbird` file is a small header, the song and layer settings as JSON, and each layer's sound as 16-bit samples (a looped layer stores one pass), delta-coded, split into high and low bytes, and gzipped, which comes to about a fifth of the raw size. Installed as an app on a computer, Songbird registers as the opener for `.songbird` files.

Instruments are real recordings. Trumpet, trombone, tuba, and French horn, held and short notes, come from [VSCO 2 Community Edition](https://github.com/sgossner/VSCO-2-CE) by Versilian Studios, released under CC0 (see `site/sounds/LICENSE-VSCO2CE.txt`); `tools/build-brass.py` rebuilds them. A horn section is those players split by range. The rest are samples rendered from the FluidR3 GM soundfont by Frank Wen, released under the MIT license (see `site/sounds/LICENSE-FluidR3.txt`); `tools/build-sounds.py` rebuilds them. Only the notes a part plays are downloaded, the first time it's used.

`node check-packages.js` checks the genre packages and the sound engine.

Genre packages are Songbird's own. They are informed by open music references, which are credited here and are not bundled into the page:

- [Open Music Theory V2](https://open.umn.edu/opentextbooks/textbooks/1254)
- [Music Theory for the 21st-Century Classroom](https://open.umn.edu/opentextbooks/textbooks/1010)
- [music21](https://music21.org/music21docs/)
- [librosa](https://librosa.org/doc/latest/)
- [Ableton Learning Music](https://learningmusic.ableton.com/)
- [Berklee: Writing and Analyzing Hit Songs](https://online.berklee.edu/courses/writing-and-analyzing-hit-songs)

Type on the page is [Plus Jakarta Sans](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Plus+Jakarta+Sans). Icons are Google's [Material Symbols](https://fonts.google.com/icons) (Apache License 2.0), and buttons follow [Material Design 3](https://m3.material.io/).

How those packages become sound, and the rest of the product design, stay with the Songbird team.
